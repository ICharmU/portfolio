Coursework at UCSD

Many of the assigned titles describe what I learned rather than official course titles.

Data Science

  • DSC 106 / Intro to UI/UX (FA25) - Most of the time spent in this class was making your visualizations feel smooth since they were web based. The emphasis was not on visualization techniques. This course taught me that if you have a high level understanding then vibe coding with Claude can produce decent results.
  • DSC 140A / Probabilistic Modeling (FA25) - Covered the math behind regression models. Felt kind of similar to DSC 106 where the material was not very fleshed out. This class was not enjoyable--the lectures were straightforward, but homeworks were hardly related. All the new/fun models (LDA, QDA, GMM) were barely covered at the end. Would not recommend taking.
  • CSE 158R / Recommender Systems (FA25) - I mostly used this class to become comfortable with implementing sklearn models and going through general model lifecycles. I wouldn't consider this much of an ML class, but it made me want to learn more about matrix factorization and linear algebra in general.
  • DSC 80 / Practice of Data Science (S225) - Great content, felt similar to DSC 20 in terms of the learning curve. Solidified foundations in pandas, numpy and sklearn for data manipulation and inference.
  • DSC 30 / Intro to DSA (SP25) - Not a very rigorous or enjoyable class. Learned a little bit of Java.
  • DSC 40B / Algorithms (SP25) - Very fun class. Learned about complexity and graph search algos.
  • DSC 20 / Python Programming (WI25) - My favorite class, I fundamentally relearned Python. Marina is an S tier prof.
  • DSC 40A / Intro to Math for ML (WI25) - Decent class. I forgot all my linear algebra, but this was a useful primer for ML classes.
  • DSC 10 / Intro to Data Science (FA24) - Not too difficult, but made me I realize I need to focus more.

Math

  • MATH 173A / Convex Optimization (FA25) - Amazing class. I can't believe this isn't a major requirement. Would recommend everyone take this. Covered convexity and loss functions for basic ML models (e.g. logistic regression for MNIST). This felt like an extension of multivariable calculus with vector derivatives which was fun to learn. The TA, Nick Karris, made this class what it was (best TA I've had thus far). He helped interpret the theorems so you could understand what was going on at a high level.
  • MATH 189 / EDA and Inference (S225) - Bootstrap bootstrap bootstrap. Conceptually stimulating class. Learned a lot of different test statistics and how to form similar heuristics.
  • MATH 180A / Intro to Probability (WI25) - A more rigorous calculus-based intro to probability.
  • MATH 109 / Intro to Proofs (FA24) - Learned formal math notation and some foundational proof techniques.
  • MATH 183 / Intro to Probability and Stats (S224) - Application of probability and statistics. Not much emphasis on derivation.

Miscellaneous

  • LIGN 101 / Survey of Linguistics (FA25) - Alright class. The amount of ambiguity and lack of mathematical understanding for the linguistic structures covered made me realize linguistics is treated as an art and not a science. Glad I took the course, but I don't think it offered much more than Crash Course's video series.
  • MGT 180 / Business Finance (SP25) - Learned a lot of financial lingo. Financial modeling in Excel.
  • USP 25 / Intro to Real Estate (FA24) - Learned Real Estate terminology and Excel.
